# About

Self-Hosting 101 is a free series from [Webnestify Education](https://webnestify.org), a Slovak non-profit I founded in 2026 to teach digital safety to anyone who needs it: schools, families, seniors, and people who would rather run their own software than rent it. This repository is the written half of the series. The videos are on the [Webnestify channel](https://youtube.com/@webnestify).

## Who I am

I'm Simon Gajdosik. I run [Webnestify](https://webnestify.cloud), a managed hosting, cloud, and security company in Bratislava, and I have spent years building and hardening servers for agencies and small businesses, on their own hardware and on providers they choose. I also co-host The Human Perimeter, a podcast about the people side of security, with André Daus.

## Why this series

Cloud prices went up hard, and one dedicated server now does the work of a fleet of cloud VMs for a fraction of the bill. I want people to run that server with trusted open source software and without spending their nights worrying about open ports. The stack in these guides is the one I run myself, and when I switch a tool I say why. Where a guide tells an AI agent to do the work, the scripts carry the safety checks, and you keep the keys and the approvals.

The education is free for everyone, and nothing in it is held back for paying customers. That is what the channel is for. If it helped you and you want to give something back, you can [donate to Webnestify Education](https://webnestify.org/donate/). Entirely up to you. Every euro lands on a bank account anyone can inspect, and the [transparency page](https://webnestify.org/transparency/) shows where it goes.

The videos run YouTube ads. I know how that sounds from someone who blocks ads on every device I own and runs AdGuard on the router. It is a small income that pays for real things, and it costs you nothing but a skip button. If you would rather not see them, an ad blocker or YouTube Premium both work, and I will not think less of you for either.

## The line between the company and the non-profit

How Webnestify Education is funded, and how it relates to my company, is on the [transparency page](https://webnestify.org/transparency/), not here. Free talks and workshops for schools, universities, and communities come through the non-profit. If an institution then wants paid implementation, my company does that work under a separate contract.

My company is not paying for this series and gets no placement in it. If a link in this repository ever earns a commission, the page says so next to the link.

If you would rather not run this yourself, or you run a business and want ownership of your data without the night shift, that is what my company does. The deal is the same as in these guides: you own everything. You get sudo on your servers if you ask for it, and if you leave, you leave with the websites and the data. No gatekeeping. That is a separate conversation, and it is not what this repository is for.
